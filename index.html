          Build your own scenario

          This is the same model behind my five scenarios. Every input starts at Bangladesh’s real value, so if you change nothing you get my base case. Change any of them and your scenario redraws on the chart above.

          The model works year by year. Each year it adds new investment to the country’s stock of machines and infrastructure, takes away what has worn out, grows the workforce, and applies productivity growth. Then it does the same again for the next year, all the way to FY2040-41.

          The growth model in one line:

          Output = Productivity × Capital × Skilled workers

          Capital and workers are things you can count. Productivity is how well a country combines them, and it’s the one input nobody can measure directly.

            About my five scenarios

            Why only productivity changes

            Capital and labour can be counted, so they use Bangladesh’s real data in all five scenarios. Productivity can’t be counted. My base case of 0.53% is what’s left over once the last decade of actual growth is explained by everything measurable. The other scenarios bracket it with the World Bank’s own 0%, 1% and 2% benchmarks, plus a climate case using the Delta Plan’s damage estimate.

            Why growth slows over time

            Investment stays high, so capital keeps piling up, but each extra unit adds less than the one before. The model tracks this every year, so the slowdown comes from the arithmetic rather than an assumption.

            Limits worth knowing

            Population growth is held at its recent average, though fertility is falling. Climate damage is applied as a flat cut to productivity. Treat every figure as a scenario, not a forecast.
